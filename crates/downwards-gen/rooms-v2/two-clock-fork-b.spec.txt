# two-clock-fork-b -- medium variant of "Two-Clock Fork (one crossing, two honest routes)".
#
# One crossing (west <-> east), two visibly different lanes that both work, each
# governed by its own clock. The lanes never share a hazard, so which lane is
# cheaper depends entirely on when you arrive at the entry bay.
#
#   LOW LANE (no ability, short, fast clock): the open floor row 16 from the
#     entry bay straight to the reconvergence bay. Two pistons at cols 11-13 and
#     cols 20-22 share period 70 / active 24 but sit half a period apart (phase 0
#     and 35), so they beat one-two-one-two and the lane must be threaded at a
#     run with a beat of rest in the pocket between them.
#
#   HIGH LANE (dash-gated, long, slow clock): the row-10 ledge, cols 4-25. It is
#     reached by the west chimney (one-way rungs at rows 15/13/11) onto the pad at
#     col 4, and then through a ONE-TILE slot at row 9, cols 5-6 -- 10px of
#     headroom against a 12px player, so it is a dash-crawl and nothing else. The
#     ledge sits 70px above the low floor with no opposing walls beneath it, well
#     past a 40px jump, so there is no second way up. Its single hazard is one
#     wide 60px curtain, period 200 / active 70: one long, unmistakable window.
#
# The medium twist (variant axes "route length vs window generosity" x "one route
# coin-rich, the other fast"): the fork is NOT symmetric in payoff. The low lane
# is the fast crossing and carries one easy coin; the high lane is the slow,
# ability-taxed one and carries two thirds of the room's gold, including the
# bonus, so the choice is speed-vs-greed and not merely execution-vs-patience.
# 70 and 200 never line up inside a crossing, so the two clocks are read
# separately -- a player who waits at the entry bay is choosing, not stalling.
#
# The chimney/pad is deliberately visitable without dash: a bootless player can
# climb up, look through the slot at the high lane, and come back down. The gate
# is seen, not guessed.
