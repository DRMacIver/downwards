# Winch Stair: the service stair wrapped round the winch drum.
#
# Authored 2026-08-17 for the dungeon portfolio's loadout axis. The
# vocabulary had four west-east-ceiling junctions (chimney-lock, both
# keyhole vaults, low-ceiling-arena-c) and NOT ONE of them can be
# crossed door-to-door bare-handed: every three-door junction in the
# set demands a wall jump or a dash somewhere. That is fine for a
# dungeon built around traversal pickups and fatal for one built
# without them, so the skeleton search could not seat a single
# junction cell in a no-item dungeon. This room is that missing piece:
# a junction whose every ordered pair is a walk, so the ascent is a
# matter of reading the stair rather than owning the kit.
#
# THE DRUM. Columns 1-11 and 20-30, rows 1-12, are the winch housing:
# solid stone, no way through, no way in. It exists to make the room a
# genuine T-junction instead of an open hall — the bottom passage and
# the roof are connected by exactly one thing, the stair, and the
# stair hangs dead centre so both side doors pay the same toll for the
# roof. (The first cut of this room put the stair in the east shaft
# and the bare climb from the west door ran past the solver's horizon:
# a junction nobody can prove is crossable is not a junction.)
#
# THE BOTTOM PASSAGE (rows 13-16, both side doors). A 30-tile run
# under the drum. It is not empty: a two-tile mound at cols 4-5 breaks
# the west stride, and two spike beds (cols 8-9 and 22-23) sit either
# side of the stair's foot. The reward coin sits on the floor
# BETWEEN them, so taking it means clearing a bed and then clearing it
# again on the way back — the room's cheapest coin still costs two
# committed hops, and the passage reads as a place rather than a
# corridor.
#
# THE STAIR (central shaft, cols 12-19). Six one-way treads in two
# alternating bands, 20px apart, starting with a 30px step up out of
# the passage and ending on a tread directly under the roof hatch. Alternating bands mean every step is a small sideways
# decision as well as a jump, and one-ways mean the descent is the
# same stair taken by pressing down — the room is symmetric in use
# without being symmetric in shape.
#
# THE COUNTERWEIGHT (hazard 1) drops through the east band of the
# shaft on a 132-tick cycle, 44 of them lethal. It covers one tread
# and the air above it and nothing else, so the climb has a rhythm: safe step, timed
# step, safe step, timed step. There is a full tread to stand and wait
# on before each timed one — resynchronisation geometry, not a
# one-tick window.
#
# THE GRIT FALL (hazard 2) meters the passage itself, 60 ticks out of
# phase with the counterweight: it lands on the floor between the
# mound and the stair's foot, so the walk to the stair is a beat and
# the climb is the opposite beat. Together they give the room its
# character: the way out of the top of the dungeon is metered, and the
# meter is legible from the passage floor, where you can stand still
# for as long as you like.
