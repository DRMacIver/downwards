# Sump Corner: the gallery over the sump, with an overflow loft nobody
# has to visit.
#
# Authored 2026-08-17 for the portfolio's loadout axis. West-floor
# corners are the shape a descent needs most (a passage that turns
# down) and the vocabulary had exactly one that a bare-handed player
# can cross, greed-loop-b, which the no-repeat rule then spends on the
# first such cell in the dungeon. This is the second, and where the
# greed loop is a lap with a bank, this one is a decision about how
# much of the room you are willing to climb.
#
# THE FLOOR OF IT (rows 13-16). The west door opens onto a short
# gallery; the hatch sits six tiles east of it under a one-way sill,
# with a spike pair at cols 7-8 in between, so even the shortest
# possible route through the room — walk in, drop out — is a hop and
# then a timed press-down. Two counter-phased drain gates (hazards 1
# and 2) flood the floor either side of the sill on a 108-tick cycle:
# the fastest exit from this room is the one thing in it you cannot
# rush, and the sill you leave from is the one dry tile.
#
# THE STACK (rows 1-12). Three one-way floors — cols 1-12 at rows 12
# and 4, cols 19-30 at row 8 — braid a climb out of the sump that
# crosses the room twice. It is entirely optional: nothing above row
# 13 is on any route between the two doors. What is up there is money,
# arranged so that the deeper you climb the further from the doors you
# have to walk: the mid-gallery coin sits at the east end of the
# second storey, the loft coin at the east end of the third, and the
# overflow sluice (hazard 2) cuts the mid-gallery run at cols 19-24 on
# a 132-tick cycle out of phase with the drain gate below.
#
# THE POINT. In a coin-gated dungeon this room is where a player
# decides whether the gate is close enough to skip the loft, and it
# reads that way from the door: you can see the stack, you can see it
# is a dead end, and you can see it is three storeys. Descent rooms
# that only descend make the map feel like a slide; one that offers a
# climb you may refuse makes the coin gate a live question.
