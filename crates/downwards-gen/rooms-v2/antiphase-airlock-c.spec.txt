# antiphase-airlock-c -- HARD variant of "Antiphase Airlock".
#
# THE TRAVELLING WAVE. Four partitions (cols 6, 12, 18, 24) cut the room into
# five chambers, each with unbroken standing floor -- every chamber is a bank
# point. The shutters do NOT strictly alternate: their phases march DOWN the
# room (72 / 48 / 24 / 0 west to east), so the safe window rolls eastward one
# gate every 24 ticks. An eastbound player who launches on the beat can ride a
# single wave through all four gates in one continuous sprint; a westbound
# player runs into the wave head-on and must bank a full cycle in every
# chamber. Advance and retreat are both possible, but they are different
# rhythms, and that asymmetry is the hard expression of the class rather than
# "more gates, faster".
#
# ACTIVE EXCEEDS HALF THE PERIOD: 66 lethal of 120, so each gate is shut more
# often than it is open (54-tick window). There is not one spike in the room
# and not one gap wider than a walk -- the floor at row 17 is unbroken and
# every gate is a flat 20px-high run-through. Danger is purely temporal.
#
# THE AIRLOCK ITSELF (col 18, the middle partition). This gate has TWO valves
# in strict antiphase with each other: an UPPER valve at rows 12-13 over a
# solid slab at row 14, and a LOWER valve at rows 15-16 at floor level. Phases
# 24 and 84 are exactly half a period apart, so the two valves are NEVER open
# together and there is a 6-tick beat where both are shut. Passing the middle
# of the room means choosing a lane on the beat, and the lanes are 30px apart
# vertically: a step block (col 16 / col 20, row 16) and a ledge (col 17 /
# col 19, row 14) are the only way between them, two 20px hops. Guess the lane
# wrong and you pay a full cycle re-climbing. That is the room's thesis: a real
# airlock, where the inner door is only open when the outer one is not.
door west
door east
