# Tide Shaft (variant b, medium) -- "the converging tide"
#
# CONCEPT. A ceiling-to-floor shaft crossed by five full-width hazard bands on
# tile rows 2, 5, 8, 11 and 14, with a stack of one-way shelves hanging exactly
# one row beneath each band (rows 3, 6, 9, 12, 15). A shelf is therefore only
# standable while its own band is dark -- the tide does not merely block the
# lanes, it decides which shelf you are allowed to be alive on. Falling is the
# free part of this room; standing still is the expensive part.
#
# THE MEDIUM TWIST -- DOUBLE TIDE. The class sketch marches one band downward.
# This variant takes the "two bands travelling in opposite directions that
# cross mid-room once per cycle" axis instead: two fronts run at once, one
# stepping DOWN from row 2 and one stepping UP from row 14, sharing period 180
# and one row per 36 ticks. They meet on row 8 -- the middle of the shaft --
# once per cycle. Written out, the shaft breathes:
#     t   0- 30   rows  2 and 14 lit   (the outer shelves burn)
#     t  36- 66   rows  5 and 11 lit
#     t  72-102   row   8 lit          (THE MEETING -- only the middle burns)
#     t 108-138   rows  5 and 11 lit   (the fronts separate again)
#     t 144-174   rows  2 and 14 lit
# with a 6-tick slack-water gap between every step. So the safe pocket does
# not travel in one direction: it collapses inward and then blows back out.
# A descender cannot simply ride ahead of the water. They drop into the
# closing half of the cycle, stop, let a front pass over them, drop again --
# and the direction they have to out-wait REVERSES halfway down the shaft.
#
# GEOMETRY, and why nothing here is a spike corridor.
#  * THE MEETING STONE: solid slab, rows 8-9, cols 12-19. It occupies row 8 --
#    the one row both fronts share -- so the tide's collision point is walled
#    off, and its top surface (y=80) sits one row ABOVE the row-8 band. It is
#    the only mid-shaft place that is safe in every phase: the refuge you aim
#    for when you have mistimed a beat, and the landmark that makes the whole
#    clock readable. It also plugs the free plummet from ceiling mouth to
#    floor mouth, so the shaft must be taken shelf by shelf.
#  * DOOR-MOUTH NOTCHES: the outer bands (rows 2 and 14) are cut away over
#    cols 14-17. The top notch turns the row-3 centre perch under the ceiling
#    into an always-safe observation deck -- land, watch one full cycle, then
#    commit -- and the bottom notch keeps the last 40px of the drop into the
#    floor sill out of the tide. Observe, commit, land.
#  * The side shelves are staggered left/right so each 3-row drop is also a
#    lateral steer, and the three coins are all out on shelf tips, i.e. inside
#    their band's own row. Every coin is a bank-on-beat.
#
# Spikes at row 16 cols 11-12 and 19-20 flank the floor sill: overshooting the
# final drop is fatal, undershooting only costs a re-climb, per the playbook.
#
# LOADOUT. None. Every ordered door pair, including both self-pairs, solves
# with no abilities; the room is pure clock-reading and steering. Wall and
# dash only shorten the coin detours.
