# greed-loop-c -- hard variant of "Greed Loop", built on the class's
# figure-eight axis: TWO rings that share one bank tile.
#
# The room is a lying-down 8. A central chute (cols 12-19, full height) is the
# waist; two solid islands (cols 4-11 and 20-27, rows 5-13) are the two holes.
# Each ring is run in one direction only:
#
#   BANK (the one-way bridge, cols 14-17 row 16, directly over the floor door)
#     -> hop the flanking spikes into the bottom gutter, left or right
#     -> climb that side's shaft on a 3-tile-pitch one-way rung stack
#        (rows 14 / 11 / 8 / 5 at cols 1-3 or cols 28-30)
#     -> run inward along that side's top corridor, THROUGH ITS SHUTTER
#     -> the corridor dead-ends at the chute mouth: step off and free-fall
#        eleven rows back onto the bank bridge.
#
# THE BANK RULE, literal: the bank tile is the one-way bridge over the floor
# door, so banking is pressing down on it and leaving the room. Everything else
# is lap. The bridge is 40px wide and flanked by 20px of spikes on BOTH sides
# (cols 12-13 and 18-19, row 16) -- it is the return drop's landing pad, so an
# aborted lap is emphatically not free: you must thread the drop, and every
# departure from the bank starts with a 30px spike clear. Those two spike
# flanks are the room's ability gate and they are the first thing you see: a
# plain jump does not cross 30px of spikes from a standing step-down, so the
# room is DASH-REQUIRED door-to-door. Both self-pairs still solve at every
# loadout, so a player bounced off a locked door can always retreat.
#
# WHAT MAKES IT THE HARD EXPRESSION (not a scaled-up easy lap):
#  * Two rings, not one. The greedy player does not run a longer lap, he runs
#    a DIFFERENT SHAPE of lap -- and to chain both rings in one go he must take
#    the crossing platform, which is the only link between the two top
#    corridors and hangs over an 80px hole.
#  * The two shutters are 90 ticks out of phase, so which ring you enter first
#    is a timing read, and a two-ring lap has to beat both gates.
#  * Falling anywhere in the room ends at the bank -- alive, and holding
#    nothing you did not already have. The punishment for greed is the lap you
#    just spent, not a death screen. That is the class's economy made physical.
door west
door floor

# COIN 0 -- lap depth ~1/8, right ring, plain gutter walk east of the bank.
# The only coin behind no toll: it exists to prove coins live on the ring.
coin 240 150

# COIN 1 -- lap depth ~1/2, left ring, hanging over the third rung of the left
# shaft. Taking it means stopping mid-ladder on a one-way stack you cannot
# comfortably climb back down, so the grab commits you to finishing the left
# lap over the top and through the left shutter.
coin 15 100

# COIN 2 -- the deepest coin and the waist of the eight. It sits on the
# crossing platform (cols 14-17, row 3), a one-way slab suspended in the mouth
# of the chute with eleven empty rows beneath it. Reaching it is a 30px gap
# jump out over the hole from either top corridor, and the platform is also the
# ONLY way to convert a one-ring lap into a both-rings lap. Miss it and you do
# not die -- you fall the whole chute and land at the bank with whatever you
# were already carrying, which is exactly the trade the class is about.
coin 150 20

# SHUTTERS -- one per ring, in the 2-tile-high top corridors, each the sole
# gate between that ring's ladder and the chute mouth. 60 shut of every 180
# ticks, phase 0 on the left and phase 90 on the right: the tolls alternate, so
# a player who wants both rings in one lap has to pick the side that is opening
# and accept that the other gate will have swung shut by the time he crosses.
hazard 80 30 10 20 180 60 0
hazard 230 30 10 20 180 60 90
