# Lantern Cross (deep): the crossing under the seal.
#
# Same family job as the airy variant - a crossroads, not a test, every door
# pair readable and mostly bare - but cut out of rock instead of hung in
# air. The middle of the room is one solid keep from row 4 to row 12, and
# the four doors are stitched around it rather than through it. All the
# vertical traffic is pushed out to two wells hard against the side walls
# (cols 1-6 and cols 25-30); all the horizontal traffic runs in two flat
# corridors, the keep roof (rows 1-3, with the ceiling mouth in it) and the
# low gallery (rows 13-15, with the west and east mouths in it). That is the
# whole silhouette: a block with a hole in its floor, a ladder at each
# shoulder, and one chimney bored down through it off-centre.
#
# Up and down are deliberately not the same route, and they are not on the
# same side. From west or east you climb the well at your own shoulder -
# five staggered shingles, an honest 20px a hop, alternating cols 1-3/4-6
# and 25-27/28-30 - step onto the keep roof and walk inward to the plinth
# under the ceiling mouth. Coming the other way you do not climb down at
# all: the seal shaft at cols 18-19 is a bare 20px chimney bored clean
# through the keep, capped at roof level by a one-way grate, so from the
# roof you simply press down and fall the whole height of the room onto the
# lip beside the sump. Down the middle, up the sides.
#
# That is also the room's gate. The floor door is a grating at the centre of
# the low gallery - cols 14-15 are a rusted plate you land on, cols 16-17
# are open air, so arriving from below you stand with a 20px hole at your
# right hand and every careless step east drops you back out of the room.
# West and east are a short walk from there. The ceiling is not: the seal
# shaft is smooth, so getting back up means going all the way out to a
# shoulder, up the well, and all the way back in along the roof, with a
# flue to time in whichever direction you take it. The dungeon's soft-lock
# analysis depends on that dogleg staying too long to walk, so the keep
# stays solid. Wall jumps turn the seal shaft itself into the way home
# (93 ticks instead of never), and a dash makes the dogleg cheap (133).
#
# The low gallery is not quite level: cols 18-19 stand 10px proud of the
# rest of the floor - the foot of the seal shaft - so the sump reads as a
# step down coming west and a step up going east, and you always know which
# side of the hole you are on.
#
# Hazards: two flues, one either side of the sump (cols 8-11 and cols
# 20-23), full height of the low gallery, 180-tick period and only 36 ticks
# hot, running 90 ticks out of phase with each other. Crossing the room is a
# two-beat stroll rather than a gate - the deep answer to the lantern pulse
# upstairs, exhaling instead of glowing - and neither flue stands between
# the seal shaft and the sump, so the honest descent is never timed.
#
# Coins - none of the three sits on a mandatory line.
#  - coin 0 is on the keep roof at its west lip (col 7), the far end of the
#    roof from the east well and one lip past the ceiling mouth from the
#    west one.
#  - coin 1 is in the west gallery, a shelf cut into the keep's flank at
#    rows 6-8, entered by a 30px side-jump off the cols 1-3 shingle near the
#    top of the west well and dead-ending against solid at col 11.
#  - coin 2 is the mirror shelf on the east flank, entered by jumping inward
#    off the cols 28-30 shingle and dead-ending at col 20 against the wall
#    of the seal shaft. Both shelves are blind from the low gallery, so the
#    crossroads keeps its secrets at shoulder height.
