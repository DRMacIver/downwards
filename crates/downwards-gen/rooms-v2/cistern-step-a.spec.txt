# Cistern Step: the drained cistern with a stepped rim and a chimney.
#
# Authored 2026-08-17 alongside winch-stair-a for the same reason: of
# the two west-ceiling-floor junctions in the vocabulary only one
# (greed-loop-a) can be crossed bare, so a dungeon with no traversal
# items had a single grid to seat every such cell and the no-repeat
# rule then made those skeletons unfillable. This is the second, and
# it is deliberately a different animal from the greed loop: no lap,
# no bank, just a tank you climb out of.
#
# THE TANK (cols 1-18, rows 3-15). Six one-way rim steps in two
# alternating bands, 20px apart: cols 12-17 at rows 4, 8, 12 and cols
# 4-9 at rows 6, 10, 14. Bare-handed the climb is six deliberate steps
# with a full ledge to stand on between each; the descent is the same
# steps taken by pressing down, or one straight drop if you are in a
# hurry, because the rim steps are one-ways and the basin below is
# open floor. The ceiling hatch sits directly over the top step, so
# the last step and the exit are the same jump.
#
# THE DRAIN (floor hatch, cols 14-17). A one-way sill at row 16 spans
# the hatch: you land on it coming in from below, you press down on it
# going out. Two counter-phased surges (hazards 1 and 2) flood the
# basin floor either side of it, so reaching the drain is a timed
# commitment from either direction while the sill itself stays dry —
# the safe tile is the one you are trying to leave from.
#
# THE CHIMNEY (cols 28-30, rows 3-11). Thirty pixels of bare facing
# wall from the east basin up to the top corridor, no ledge in it. A
# wall-jumper climbs it and skips the entire rim; bare-handed it is a
# way DOWN only, and taking it commits you to the bottom of the tank.
# That is the room's loadout gradient: same grid, three different
# shapes depending on what you are carrying, and no crossing that
# needs an ability to complete.
#
# THE RIM SHUTTER (hazard 3) cuts the gap between the two step bands
# at cols 10-11, half a beat out of phase with the drain. Every
# band-to-band step in the lower half of the tank is therefore taken
# on a beat, and the beat is the opposite of the one governing the way
# out — the room asks you to hold two clocks, which is what makes it
# worth a junction cell in a hard dungeon.
